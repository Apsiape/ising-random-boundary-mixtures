\section{Temperature margins and numerical checks}\label{sec:constants}
The contour sum has the closed form
\[
 \sum_{\ell\ge4}4(\ell+2)3^{\ell-1}e^{-2K\ell}
 =\frac43\sum_{\ell\ge4}(\ell+2)r^\ell
 =\frac43r^4\frac{6-5r}{(1-r)^2}.
\]
It is strictly increasing in $r\in(0,1)$ because every coefficient in its series is positive, and it runs from zero to infinity. As $r=3e^{-2K}$ strictly decreases with $K$, there is a unique $K_P>\tfrac12\log3$ with $B(K_P)=1/2$. Equivalently its $r$-coordinate is the unique root in $(0,1)$ of
\begin{equation}\label{eq:poly}
 8r^4(6-5r)=3(1-r)^2.
\end{equation}
The threshold exceeds $K_c$, since $\tfrac12\log3>K_c$.

Here are all the numerical margins used in the proof. Choose $t=(2K-\log3)/2$, so the good-cap and long-curve ratio is $\rho=e^{-t}<1$. For short arcs, the conditional mean is eventually at most $t\ell/2$ and the bad-ratio exponent is $t^2\ell/(64K^2b^2)$. For long curves, the global allowance $n^{7/10}$ is $o(n^{3/4})$ and hence at most $t\ell$. The global disorder exponent remains $n^{2/5}/(288K^2b^2)$.

With $g=1-2B(K)>0$, each expected remainder below $g/8$ gives expected phase separation at least $3g/4$. In the fair clipping argument, a good position has gap at least $g/2$; any bad position forces a remainder sum larger than $g/2$. The depth is chosen after the desired exceptional probability. For buffered blocks, first choose $L$ to make jumps smaller than the target window. With $a=2^{-2L}$, then choose each expected remainder below $ag/8$. This gives active expected separation at least $3ag/4$. Next choose $A$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:blockdrift}, and finally the lower size cutoff. The connected-line perimeter ratio needs only $3e^{-2K}<1$. Spatial relaxation and wall uniqueness require $K>K_c$, already satisfied. Thus no additional colder-temperature inequality enters the argument.

At $K=\log4$ the useful checks are exact:
\[
 r=\frac3{16},\quad B(K)=\frac{2187}{173056}<\frac1{25},\quad
 \epsilon_K=\frac1{65537},\quad
 \kappa_K=\frac1{257}-\frac1{65537}>0,
\]
\[
 1-e^{-4K}=\frac{255}{256},\qquad e^{4K}-1=255.
\]
The equality for $\kappa_K$ follows by evaluating the four heat-bath increments: the smallest two are the symmetric increments at the extreme fields. The condition $B(K)<1/2$ then follows without rounding.

The threshold decimal can be reproduced by bisection of the rational polynomial \eqref{eq:poly} on $(0,1)$, followed by $K_P=\tfrac12\log(3/r)$. Rational evaluation certifies the bracket for $r$; evaluation of the logarithm gives the displayed decimal approximation. These numerical values illustrate the exact range, whose proof is the monotonicity and strict inequality above. They do not approximate a finite-volume Gibbs distribution. The proofs use no simulated evidence.
