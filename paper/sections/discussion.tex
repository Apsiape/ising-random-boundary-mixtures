\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
A fixed neighbourhood of an interior mixture is visited infinitely often by Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}, while its empirical frequency is zero by Theorem~\ref{thm:laws} whenever its closure avoids the pure phases. This is the null recurrence of mixed states anticipated in \cite{EMN02}, \cite[Section~12]{vENS05} and \cite[Remark~3.2]{vENS06}; its zero-frequency half is \cite[Theorem~3.4]{vENS05}, which those authors already describe as the null-recurrent character of the mixed states. The cluster set is a closed segment of Gibbs measures, and the empirical metastate is the equal two-atom measure on its endpoints. Its barycentre is the symmetric Gibbs state. These three objects therefore encode different information.

Theorem~\ref{thm:sharp} gives the $O(N^{-1/2})$ upper bound conjectured in \cite[Remark~3.6]{vENS05} for every $K>\log2$, including $K\ge1$, with fair independent bulk-strength boundary spins. Together with the lower bound it gives exact order $N^{-1/2}$ for every fixed finite $X$ and $0<\eps<\tfrac12\|\mu^+-\mu^-\|_X$, and for every fixed interior calibrated-weight interval. The full-sequence recurrence conjecture and this quantitative conjecture are therefore both resolved, the latter in that temperature range and with the necessary separation threshold. A literature search found no earlier proof of the upper bound. The result does not assert a Gaussian local limit or an asymptotic equivalent.

Read as a two-sided order statement for every $\eps>0$, Remark~3.6 of \cite{vENS05} does not hold above $\tfrac12\|\mu^+-\mu^-\|_X$. This qualification follows from the geometry of the mixture segment: its midpoint maximizes the distance to the nearer endpoint. Lemma~\ref{lem:seminormtransfer} makes larger departures stretched-exponentially unlikely. The sharp statement is the upper bound for every positive separation and the matching lower bound below that midpoint value. At the threshold itself we claim only the upper bound.

The recurrence, lower-bound and empirical-law proofs work at every $K>K_c$ because their inputs are non-perturbative. Dual open arcs of large diameter have exponentially small unnormalized weight, since the dual model is subcritical. Fair boundary disorder makes every macroscopic interval reversal cost a sublinear log partition weight, so with high probability the bulk lies in one constant-sign component of the open-arc decomposition. A central majority identifies its sign with an error exponentially small in the radius $k_n$ of the central square. For arcs near the north side one can condition on the complement of a strip, which keeps the majority event fixed and makes the same comparison available for the constrained partition functions. The heat-bath response and wall uniqueness then give the fixed-window lower probability. The same estimates show that typical fair boundaries suppress macroscopic interfaces throughout the ordered phase: by Lemma~\ref{lem:long} and the summable bound \eqref{eq:H}, almost surely $\mu_n^\eta(\mathcal E_n)\le e^{-cn}$ for all large $n$. At sufficiently low temperature, van Enter, Neto\v cn\'y and Schaap proved that a typical boundary condition suppresses interfaces \cite[Theorem~4.1]{vENS05}; see also \cite[Remark~3.3]{vENS06}.

The proof separates two scales of boundary response. Under a near-fair product bias, a linear number of separated phase responses gives a drift larger than $\sqrt n$. Under the buffered law, the same argument survives independent groups of fixed size, even though individual bits are dependent. Uniform-wall uniqueness makes the selected centre responses arbitrarily small. Their pivotal crossings then provide the local lower bound. The buffers and depths may be very large and depend on the target window. The constants are not asserted to be uniform as $K\to\infty$, as $K\downarrow K_c$, or as the window shrinks. For the sharp upper bound, joint deletion charges a family of distinct arcs through its total diameter before summing endpoint specifications. The resulting exponential coverage tail survives division by the majority mass and leaves a positive density of responding bits outside a summable disorder exception. Clipping the odds and taking covariance with the fair boundary plus count then gives the exponent $-1/2$. Corollary~\ref{cor:allsides} treats both parities, including the lower bound obtained by a uniform permutation and an independent binomial prefix.

The sharp upper bound for $K_c<K\le\log2$ remains open; the lower bound holds throughout that interval for odd side lengths. The joint deletion argument used here requires convergence of the walk sum $\sum_h4^he^{-2Kh}$, and so does not reach it. A density asymptotic and a limiting prefactor also remain open. Two further questions are the weak-coupling model of van Enter, Medved' and Neto\v cn\'y \cite{EMN02}, whose boundary bonds are weaker than the bulk bonds, and three dimensions. In the weak-coupling model the uniform-wall uniqueness of Section~\ref{sec:model} and the homogeneity of the dual model are no longer available. In three dimensions, \cite[Remark~3.2]{vENS05} expects the cluster set to be the set of all translation-invariant Gibbs measures, and the planar classification and dual-line argument used here do not apply.
