\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}
A fixed neighbourhood of an interior mixture is visited infinitely often by Theorem~\ref{thm:cluster}, while its empirical frequency is zero by Theorem~\ref{thm:laws} whenever its closure avoids the pure phases. This is the null recurrence of mixed states anticipated in \cite{EMN02}, \cite[Section~12]{vENS05} and \cite[Remark~3.2]{vENS06}; its zero-frequency half is \cite[Theorem~3.4]{vENS05}, which those authors already describe as the null-recurrent character of the mixed states. The cluster set is a closed segment of Gibbs measures, and the empirical metastate is the equal two-atom measure on its endpoints. Its barycentre is the symmetric Gibbs state. These three objects therefore encode different information.

The quantitative contribution is the lower bound for each arbitrarily narrow fixed window. Together with \cite[Proposition~3.5]{vENS05}, in the common temperature regime it places an interior-window probability between $c n^{-1/2}$ and $C_\alpha n^{-1/2+\alpha}$. To check the comparison, a fixed interval of weights with closure in $(0,1)$ stays a positive origin-marginal distance from each endpoint, so it is included in the departure event of that proposition. Their squares $\Lambda(N)$ coincide with our $B_N$. The result does not assert a Gaussian local limit or an asymptotic equivalent; the crossing count needs only a positive window-dependent constant.

The proof separates two scales of boundary response. Under a near-fair product bias, a linear number of separated phase responses gives a drift larger than $\sqrt n$. Under the buffered law, the same argument survives independent groups of fixed size, even though individual bits are dependent. Uniform-wall uniqueness makes the selected centre responses arbitrarily small. Their pivotal crossings then provide the local lower bound. The buffers and depths may be very large and depend on the target window. The constants are not asserted to be uniform as $K\to\infty$ or as the window shrinks.

The threshold $K_P$ comes from the explicit enclosing-contour overcount $B(K)<1/2$. At equality its guaranteed phase gap vanishes; this is a limitation of that estimate. The region $K_c<K\le K_P$, where $K_c=0.44068679\ldots$, remains open for the full-sequence conclusion proved here. Improved contour counts or a different separation argument could enlarge the proved range.
